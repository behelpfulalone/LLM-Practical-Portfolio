## Introduction

This practical portfolio demonstrates my learning throughout the Large Language Models and Generative AI module. The six laboratory activities covered traditional machine learning, generative AI, transformer architectures, model fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation, and intelligent AI agents.

## Lab 01 - Sentiment Analysis

This laboratory helped me understand text preprocessing, feature extraction, and sentiment classification. I explored Bag of Words and TF-IDF representations using logistic regression and learned how to evaluate classification performance through accuracy and confusion matrices.

## Lab 02 - LLMs in Everyday Life

Through the CampusFlow concept, I explored how large language models can support practical solutions to everyday university challenges. This activity improved my understanding of prompt engineering, AI-assisted product development, and visual prototyping.

## Lab 03 - Tiny Transformer

This laboratory improved my understanding of transformer architectures, character-level tokenization, attention mechanisms, and language model training. Comparing different model architectures helped me understand the relationship between model complexity, training performance, and text generation quality.

## Lab 04 - LLM Fine-Tuning Using LoRA

This activity introduced parameter-efficient fine-tuning using LoRA. Developing the TechNova Computers assistant demonstrated the importance of dataset quality, training configuration, and evaluation. The experiments highlighted the challenges of improving domain-specific responses while maintaining generalization.

## Lab 05 - Retrieval-Augmented Generation

This laboratory involved developing a multi-document question-answering chatbot using Ollama, LangChain, ChromaDB, and Streamlit. I gained practical experience in document processing, vector embeddings, semantic retrieval, and grounded response generation.

The system successfully answered all eight questions in the manual evaluation. This activity demonstrated how RAG can improve response relevance and reduce unsupported answers.

## Lab 06 - AI Agents

The shared Intelligent University Assistant implementation demonstrated how language models can interact with external tools, persistent memory, university knowledge bases, and Google Workspace services.

Reviewing the implementation improved my understanding of tool selection, OAuth authentication, approval-based automation, and the limitations of AI agents. The prototype also highlighted areas requiring further development, including unified natural language routing and student record updates.
